## Size the vial to the stage you're actually on

The right tirzepatide vial size changes as a titration schedule progresses, because weekly consumption at the low starting dose is much smaller than at a later step. Buying one size for the entire schedule tends to either run out early at higher doses or leave a vial sitting half-used for weeks during the slower early stages.

### Why concentration matters more than the mg number on the label

Two vials can both say "tirzepatide" with different total mg content reconstituted to different concentrations. What matters for sizing is mg per ml, since that's what determines how many weeks a given vial actually covers at a specific weekly dose.

## Calculating what a stage actually needs

### Weekly dose times weeks held

Multiply the weekly dose for the current step by the number of weeks it's typically held (commonly one week per 1mg step, in a safe general schedule rising from 1mg to a 4mg maintenance dose) to get the total mg needed for that stage. Compare that against the vial's total mg content before ordering. Enzo Meds' customer service team can help confirm the plan you're working from.

### Add a margin for draw loss

Some product is always lost to needle dead space and preparation, so it's sensible to size a purchase around 10-15% above the bare calculated minimum rather than cutting it exactly to the number.

## Buying per stage vs buying for the whole schedule

### Smaller, more frequent purchases

Buying just enough for the stage in front of you means reconstituted tirzepatide never sits around longer than it needs to, and it keeps the option open to adjust the plan if an early stage doesn't go as expected.

### Buying ahead for convenience

Ordering two stages' worth in one go can reduce delivery costs and the chance of a gap between steps, provided the extra vials stay unreconstituted and in their intended storage condition until they're actually needed.

## What to look for in a UK listing's size options

A titration-transparent tirzepatide listing states concentration and total content per size, and ideally links each size to roughly which titration stage it's built for. Enzo Meds' tirzepatide listings are a useful example, pairing each vial size with a batch-specific COA rather than a single certificate applied across the board.

### Confirming the COA matches what's shipped

Check that the batch number on the certificate matches the batch actually being sent for the specific size ordered, since suppliers sometimes run different batches across different vial sizes of the same product.

## Practical sizing across a typical schedule

Early titration (around the first four weeks at the lowest published dose) usually needs the smallest available vial size. As the dose steps up through the middle stages, a mid-size vial avoids constant reordering. Once titration reaches the maintenance range, weekly consumption is highest, and a larger vial size tends to be the more practical and cost-effective choice.

## Common sizing mistakes to avoid

### Treating the label number as an exact usable yield

A vial marketed as containing a round mg figure doesn't always yield exactly that much once draw loss and any dead space in the vial are factored in. Use the labelled amount as a starting point for calculation, then build in the margin discussed above rather than assuming it's precise to the decimal.

### Cutting it too close on delivery timing

A correctly sized vial can still run out if a follow-up order isn't placed with enough lead time. Factor in UK delivery windows when planning the next purchase, ordering with a few days of buffer before the current vial is expected to run out.

## Why a clear supplier listing makes this easier

A supplier who states concentration, total content, and an approximate number of weekly doses a size covers takes most of the guesswork out of this calculation. This matters most for a first order, before the process of converting mg to ml becomes familiar through repetition.

## The bottom line

Work out mg needed per stage from the weekly dose and hold length, then buy the tirzepatide vial size that covers it with a small margin, rather than picking a size based on price alone. Reconstituted product left sitting unused for too long is a worse outcome than reordering slightly more often.
