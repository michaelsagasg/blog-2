## Ask whether vial sizes actually match titration stages

Start by asking whether the supplier's vial sizes are built around the stages of a typical titration schedule, or whether every size is priced and packaged the same way regardless of dose stage. A supplier who has thought about this will usually have a smaller option suited to the low starting dose and larger options for later, higher-dose stages.

### Follow up on concentration options

Ask whether the concentration (mg per ml) is fixed across all vial sizes or varies. Knowing this upfront makes it easier to plan draw volumes across an entire titration schedule rather than recalculating from scratch every time a new size is ordered.

## Ask what reference schedule they use, if any

### Where does it come from?

If a supplier publishes or references a titration schedule, ask what it's based on. A schedule referencing published trial dosing patterns is a stronger answer than one with no stated source, or one that reads as an arbitrary in-house recommendation.

### Is it framed as general information?

A supplier who's clear that any schedule they publish is general reference material, not personalised medical advice, is giving a more honest answer than one implying they can tell you exactly what dose to take.

## Ask about batch consistency across reorders

### Will a repeat order be the same batch?

Titration schedules run over months, often spanning multiple orders. Ask whether a repeat order is likely to be the same batch as a previous one, and if not, whether the new batch will come with its own COA so nothing changes silently mid-schedule.

### What happens if a batch changes mid-titration?

A supplier with a clear answer here, explaining how they'd flag a batch change and provide updated documentation, is demonstrating they've thought about what continuity actually means for someone titrating over several months.

## Ask about support if something doesn't go to plan

### What if a dose needs to be held longer than expected?

Ask whether the supplier can support a slower-than-standard titration, for example by offering smaller top-up vial sizes if a hold period needs extending. A rigid product range with no smaller options can make deviating from a fixed schedule awkward.

### What if a vial needs replacing mid-titration?

Ask what the process is if a vial is damaged, underfilled, or otherwise unusable partway through a titration stage. A supplier without a clear answer here is a weaker choice than one who has a defined replacement process.

## Ask how they handle storage guidance

Ask whether reconstitution and storage guidance is included with the order, or whether it's left to the buyer to find elsewhere. A supplier who provides this clearly, alongside the COA, is treating the purchase as part of a full titration process rather than a one-off transaction.

### A reasonable standard to compare against

Enzo Meds is one example of a UK listing that pairs batch-specific COAs with reference titration information and multiple vial sizes, which gives a useful benchmark for what a titration-aware supplier's answers should look like.

## Ask about delivery timing around stage transitions

### Can they support tight reorder windows?

A titration schedule that moves through steps every week depends on timely reorders. Ask what typical UK delivery times look like and whether the supplier can flag likely delays in advance, since a late delivery right at a stage transition is more disruptive than one earlier in a stage.

### What happens during high-demand periods?

Ask whether dispatch times slow down during particularly busy periods, and how the supplier communicates that if it happens. A supplier who's upfront about this is easier to plan around than one who only mentions it after an order is already delayed.

## Putting the answers together

No single answer to these questions makes or breaks a supplier on its own. What matters is whether the pattern across all of them, vial sizing, schedule sourcing, batch consistency, and delivery reliability, adds up to a supplier genuinely built around supporting a multi-month titration schedule, rather than one simply selling vials with no thought given to how they're actually used over time.

## The bottom line

The questions worth asking a supplier about titration support go beyond price and delivery speed: vial sizing against schedule stages, the source of any published schedule, batch consistency across reorders, and what happens if a stage needs to be extended or a vial replaced. Clear answers to these say more about a supplier than a low headline price ever will.
