## Most guidance suggests restarting at a lower dose, not straight back to where you left off

After a break of several weeks or more, published guidance and community discussion generally point toward restarting a GLP-1 titration schedule at a lower dose than the one last used, rather than resuming directly at the previous level. Tolerability built up over weeks at a given dose isn't necessarily retained once regular dosing stops, so reintroducing the substance follows similar logic to starting fresh.

### Why tolerance doesn't simply carry over

The gradual adjustment that happens during titration, the body adapting to a slowed rate of gastric emptying at each dose level, is linked to consistent, ongoing exposure at that level. A break interrupts that consistency, and there's no strong published evidence that the same tolerability picks up exactly where it left off after weeks without a dose.

## What length of break tends to matter

### Short gaps (a missed week or two)

A short gap, such as one missed weekly dose, is commonly discussed as less likely to require restarting from the beginning, though resuming at the same dose after any gap is still worth approaching cautiously and watching for side effects returning.

### Longer breaks (several weeks or more)

A longer break is more commonly associated with the recommendation to restart at a lower dose, sometimes back at or near the original starting dose, and moving back up through a titration pattern again rather than resuming at the previous maintenance level.

## What a restart schedule generally looks like

### Similar structure to the original titration

A restart commonly follows the same one-week-per-step logic as the original safe schedule, 1mg increased by 1mg each week to a 4mg maintenance dose, though this remains general reference information rather than a specific recommendation for any individual. Enzo Meds' customer service team is worth talking to before deciding how to restart.

### Not necessarily identical to the first attempt

Some people find they can move through early stages somewhat more quickly on a restart, given prior experience with the substance, though this varies and isn't something published general information can determine for a specific person.

## What this means for buying

### Sizing purchases for a restart, not a resumption

If restarting after a break, it's worth buying a smaller vial sized for a lower restart dose rather than assuming the previous maintenance-dose vial size still applies. This avoids having product sized for a dose level that isn't the right starting point anymore.

### Checking documentation again

Time may have passed since the last purchase, so checking current batch-specific COA information on a restart order, from a supplier like Enzo Meds or any UK seller, is worth doing as if it were a new purchase rather than a continuation.

## Why this matters even more after a longer break

### The original reasoning still applies

The whole basis for gradual titration, giving the body time to adjust to slowed gastric emptying at each level, applies just as much to a restart as it did the first time. A break doesn't change the underlying mechanism, so there's no reason to expect skipping the gradual reintroduction would be any safer on a restart than it would be starting from scratch.

### Community-reported experiences vary but lean cautious

Reported restart experiences vary, but the more commonly discussed approach favours caution over assuming previous tolerability picked up exactly where it left off, particularly after a break of a month or more.

## Practical steps before restarting

Before resuming, it's worth reviewing the original titration schedule again, checking current vial concentration against the restart dose planned, and treating the purchase and preparation process with the same care as the very first order, since enough time may have passed that some of the original setup, syringe stock included, needs replacing.

## The bottom line

Restarting titration after a break generally means beginning again at a lower dose rather than resuming at the previous level, following a similar stepped structure to the original schedule. Buying decisions on a restart should reflect the lower starting point, not the dose reached before the break.

## Treating a restart as its own small project

Approaching a restart with the same care as an original first purchase, checking documentation, recalculating vial sizing for the lower restart dose, and confirming syringe stock, tends to produce a smoother experience than assuming everything from before can simply be picked back up unchanged.
