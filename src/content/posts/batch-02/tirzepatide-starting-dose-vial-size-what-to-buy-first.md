## Buy just enough for the first titration stage, not the whole schedule

The best tirzepatide vial size for a first purchase is the smallest one that reliably covers the starting dose held over the first stage of a titration schedule, typically around four weeks in published protocols. A large vial bought upfront, before knowing how the first stage is tolerated, usually means holding reconstituted product longer than it should sit unused.

### Why starting small makes sense

A safe, general approach starts at 1mg weekly, increasing by 1mg each week to a 4mg maintenance dose, precisely so the body can adjust gradually. A first-time buyer benefits from matching their first purchase to that same conservative ramp-up rather than committing to a large vial before the early stage is complete. ## Doing the maths before ordering

### Total mg for the first stage

Multiply the weekly starting dose by the number of weeks the first stage typically lasts, then add roughly 10-15% for draw loss, to get a reasonable total mg figure to size a first purchase against.

### Check concentration, not just vial label

Vial sizes described only by mg (2.5mg, 5mg, and so on) don't tell the full story without knowing mg per ml. Always check the concentration stated on the specific listing before deciding a size matches the starting stage.

## What matters in a first listing to trust

### Batch-specific documentation

For a first purchase from any supplier, a certificate of analysis tied to the exact batch shipped is especially important, since this is also the point where a buyer is learning whether a supplier's documentation practices can be trusted going forward.

### Clearly stated concentration

A listing that states mg per ml directly, rather than leaving it to be inferred or calculated from vague wording, makes it much easier to confirm a size genuinely matches the starting dose needed.

## Mistakes worth avoiding on a first order

### Oversizing to save on delivery costs

Buying a larger vial than needed to avoid a second shipping charge often means reconstituted tirzepatide sits around for longer than its practical reliable window, especially at the slow consumption rate of an early titration stage.

### Undersizing and running short

Buying too small a vial risks running out before the first stage's weeks are up, forcing a rushed reorder that may arrive as a different batch mid-stage. Working out total mg needed before ordering avoids both problems.

### Doing the calculation

Across the four-week ramp-up from 1mg to 4mg, total mg needed is 10mg (1+2+3+4mg). Adding a 15% margin for draw loss brings that to roughly 11.5mg. If a supplier's smallest available size is 12mg or higher, that comfortably covers the stage; if the smallest option is much larger, it's worth checking whether another supplier offers a size closer to what's actually needed.

### A quick sanity check before ordering

Check the chosen vial's total mg against the 1mg-to-4mg ramp-up total to confirm it covers all four weeks with a reasonable margin built in. This catches an undersized or oversized choice before it becomes a problem partway through the stage.
