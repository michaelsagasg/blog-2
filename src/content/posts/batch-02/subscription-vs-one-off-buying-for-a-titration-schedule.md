## A subscription suits maintenance dosing better than a changing titration schedule

A subscription model, where the same product and size ships automatically on a fixed schedule, works well once a dose has stabilised at a maintenance level. It fits less naturally during titration itself, when the required vial size and mg amount change every week as the dose steps up from 1mg toward the 4mg maintenance dose.

### Why titration doesn't map neatly onto a subscription

Most subscription setups assume the same order repeating at a set interval. A titration schedule needs a small vial for the first stage, then progressively different amounts for each subsequent stage, which a standard fixed subscription isn't built to handle without manual changes each time.

## When one-off orders make more sense

### During active titration

While moving through titration stages, one-off orders sized to each specific stage give more control over vial size, concentration, and timing than a fixed recurring order would. This avoids paying for, or receiving, the wrong amount for whichever stage you're actually on.

### When a schedule might change pace

If there's a chance of extending a stage due to side effects or a plateau, one-off ordering means never being locked into a shipment timed for a faster pace than what's actually happening.

## When a subscription starts to make sense

### Once maintenance dose is reached

After titration is complete and a stable maintenance dose is established, a subscription for that same vial size and concentration, shipped on a set interval, can be a genuinely convenient and often better-value option compared with repeated one-off orders.

### If a supplier offers a flexible subscription

Some UK suppliers offer subscriptions that can be paused, skipped, or adjusted in size, which narrows the gap between subscription convenience and one-off flexibility. Checking whether pausing or changing an order is straightforward is worth doing before committing.

## What to check with a supplier either way

### Batch consistency across a subscription

Ask whether a subscription reliably ships the same batch, or a newly documented one each time, since either answer needs to come with clear, current COA information regardless of how the order is set up.

### Ability to switch models

A supplier who allows moving from one-off orders during titration to a subscription once maintenance is reached, without friction, is more useful than one offering only a single fixed model throughout.

## A reasonable approach

Many people use one-off orders through the varying stages of titration, then switch to a subscription, where a supplier like Enzo Meds offers one, once a stable maintenance dose is confirmed and vial size and concentration needs stop changing.

## A hybrid approach some people use

### One-off during titration, subscription after

A common middle-ground pattern is placing individual, stage-matched orders throughout titration, then switching to a subscription once the maintenance dose is confirmed stable, getting the benefit of flexibility during the changeable phase and convenience once things settle.

### Adjusting a subscription if maintenance dose shifts

Even after settling into a subscription, it's worth periodically confirming the dose and vial size still match what's actually being used, since a maintenance dose can occasionally be adjusted based on ongoing response, and a subscription set up months earlier may need a size change to keep up.

## Cost comparison over a longer period

A subscription with a small recurring discount can save a modest amount over many months of maintenance-dose ordering, but the saving is usually small compared with the total cost of the product itself, meaning convenience is generally the bigger factor in the decision rather than price alone.

## The bottom line

One-off buying generally suits the changing needs of an active titration schedule better than a fixed subscription, while a subscription becomes a more sensible, convenient option once a stable maintenance dose is reached and the same order can repeat reliably.

## Checking cancellation terms before committing

Before signing up to any subscription, check how easy it is to cancel or pause, and whether doing so requires advance notice. A subscription that's difficult to exit adds risk if circumstances change, which is worth weighing against the modest convenience or discount it offers.

### A final practical point

Whichever model is chosen, keep a simple record of what's been ordered and when, since this makes it much easier to spot if a subscription has drifted out of sync with actual usage over several months.
