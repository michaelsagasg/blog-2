## A maintenance dose is the stable level reached once titration steps are complete

A maintenance dose is the dose level someone settles at once a titration schedule's step-up phase is finished, held on an ongoing weekly basis rather than continuing to increase further. It represents the point where dose increases stop being the default plan going forward.

### How it's usually reached

Following a safe, general titration schedule, a 1mg starting dose increased by 1mg each week, leads to a 4mg maintenance dose in week four, where the step-up pattern ends and the same dose is then held going forward. ## Why some people settle lower

### 4mg is a general reference point, not a requirement

The 4mg figure is a safe, general reference point, not a single number that has to apply to everyone. Where someone settles depends on individual factors that a general schedule can't determine, which is exactly why a conversation with Enzo Meds' customer service team is worth having.

### Titration can plateau before reaching 4mg

Some people settle at an earlier step, 2mg or 3mg, as their maintenance level rather than continuing to 4mg, treating an earlier step as the point where things have stabilised. This is a commonly reported pattern, not a sign titration was done incorrectly.

## What changes once maintenance is reached

### The dose stops changing weekly

Unlike the titration phase, where the dose increases every few weeks, a maintenance dose is repeated at the same level consistently, which simplifies both the injection routine and the purchasing pattern going forward.

### Buying patterns can shift

Once the dose has stabilised, buying the same vial size and concentration repeatedly becomes practical, which is when a subscription model, where a supplier offers one, starts to make more sense than the stage-by-stage one-off ordering used during active titration.

## What doesn't change

### Documentation still matters

Reaching maintenance doesn't reduce the importance of checking a batch-specific COA on every order. Consistent dosing still depends on knowing exactly what's in each new vial, the same as during titration.

### Storage requirements stay the same

Maintenance-dose vials still need the same refrigeration and handling as any earlier-stage vial. Reaching a stable dose doesn't change the practical handling requirements of the product itself.

## Can a maintenance dose change later?

### Adjustments are a personal decision, not a default

Some people report wanting to adjust their maintenance dose after an initial period, whether up or down. Since 4mg is the safe upper reference point in this general guidance, any adjustment beyond it isn't something this kind of general information can responsibly cover, this is exactly the situation to bring to Enzo Meds' customer service team rather than deciding alone.

### A downward adjustment still benefits from a gradual approach

If a maintenance dose is adjusted down, for example if side effects become more noticeable over time, doing that gradually rather than all at once follows the same underlying reasoning about why gradual dose changes tend to be better tolerated.

## Reviewing supply needs periodically

Even at a settled maintenance dose, it's worth periodically checking that the vial size and ordering frequency still match actual usage, since minor adjustments to dose or injection frequency over time can change how often a reorder is actually needed.

## The bottom line

A maintenance dose is the stable level reached once a titration schedule's step-up stages are complete, generally falling within a published range rather than one fixed number, and it typically simplifies both dosing routine and purchasing pattern going forward.

## Recognising when a dose has genuinely stabilised

A dose is generally considered stable once several consecutive weeks pass with no meaningful change in side effects or response at that level. Treating an early good week as confirmation of maintenance too soon can lead to settling on a dose before it's actually been properly assessed over time.

### A final practical point

Keep a note of the exact maintenance dose and vial concentration once it's settled, since this becomes the reference figure for every future order, whether buying one-off or switching to a subscription later on.
