## Compare price per mg, not price per vial

The only fair way to compare retatrutide pricing across different sellers or vial sizes is price divided by total mg content, not the headline price on the listing. A cheaper-looking small vial can easily work out more expensive per mg than a larger vial from the same or a different supplier, once the maths is actually done.

### Why headline price is misleading

Vial sizes vary between suppliers, and so does concentration. A £70 vial with 5mg of retatrutide costs £14 per mg; a £120 vial with 10mg costs £12 per mg. The larger vial looks more expensive at first glance but is actually the better value per mg, which only becomes obvious once you divide price by content.

## How per-mg cost changes across a titration schedule

### Early stages use less, so per-mg cost matters less in absolute terms

At the low starting dose stage, total mg consumed over four weeks is small, so even a higher per-mg price has a limited total cost impact. This is one reason it can make sense to accept a slightly higher per-mg price for a smaller, better-suited vial size early on.

### Later stages consume more, so per-mg cost compounds

Once titration reaches higher doses, weekly consumption rises, and a higher per-mg price has a bigger total cost effect over the weeks a stage is held. This is where shopping for the best per-mg value on a larger vial size matters more.

## Doing the comparison properly

### Normalise everything to cost per mg first

Before comparing two listings, calculate cost per mg for each: total price divided by total mg content, ignoring vial volume in ml entirely, since that's not what you're actually paying for.

### Factor in COA quality, not just price

A lower per-mg price from a supplier with no batch-specific COA isn't a genuine saving if there's no way to confirm what's actually in the vial. Per-mg price comparisons are only meaningful between suppliers meeting a similar documentation standard.

## Why bulk isn't automatically cheaper per mg

Some suppliers do offer a lower per-mg price on larger vials, but not always, and it's worth checking rather than assuming. A supplier's largest size isn't guaranteed to be their best per-mg value; sometimes a mid-size option is priced more competitively.

### Weigh savings against titration stage fit

Even if a large vial has a better per-mg price, it's not a good buy if it doesn't match the current titration stage and risks sitting reconstituted too long. Per-mg savings only count if the product actually gets used within its reliable window.

## Tracking per-mg cost across a whole schedule

Keeping a simple record of per-mg price at each purchase, alongside which stage it was bought for, makes it easier to spot whether a specific supplier's pricing stays consistent across sizes or whether certain sizes are priced noticeably better than others, information that's useful for planning future stages.

## The bottom line

Compare retatrutide pricing on a per-mg basis, factoring in documentation quality and how well a vial size fits the current titration stage, rather than judging listings by their headline price alone.
