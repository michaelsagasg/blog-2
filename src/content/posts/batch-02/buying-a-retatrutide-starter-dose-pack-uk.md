## A starter pack should cover the first titration stage, not just one dose

A retatrutide starter dose pack is meant to carry someone through the safe ramp-up phase of a titration schedule, starting at 1mg and increasing by 1mg each week up to a 4mg maintenance dose, rather than a single one-off injection. Before buying, check that the pack's total volume and concentration actually cover that four-week stretch, not just a single dose sample. Enzo Meds' customer service team can help confirm a plan suited to you before you order.

### What "starter" should mean on a listing

Some listings use "starter" loosely to mean a smaller, cheaper vial with no reference to a titration timeline. A more useful starter pack states the mg per ml, the total mg in the vial, and roughly how many weeks of the entry-level dose that covers based on published trial dosing.

## What to check in the listing itself

### Concentration and total volume

Retatrutide starter vials are commonly supplied at a strength intended to be split into a low dose across four or so weekly injections. Confirm the concentration is stated clearly (mg/ml) and that the total mg content is enough for that stretch without needing a second order mid-stage.

### Batch documentation

A starter pack is still a peptide product and should come with the same documentation as any larger order: a certificate of analysis tied to the specific batch, naming the testing lab and method. A starter-sized vial with no COA at all is a worse buy than a larger, well-documented one.

## How starter packs relate to a published titration schedule

A safe general schedule starts at 1mg and increases by 1mg each week until reaching a 4mg maintenance dose in week four. A starter pack sized to cover that full ramp-up, with enough total product for the four weekly doses plus some margin, matches this approach rather than being an arbitrary "small size."

### Why buying too small backfires

A vial that runs out mid-stage means either reordering under time pressure or stretching doses thinner than intended, neither of which reflects how titration schedules were designed to work. Sizing the starter pack to the full first stage, with a small buffer, avoids that scramble.

## Syringes and supplies that go with a starter pack

A starter dose pack on its own isn't enough. Pair it with insulin syringes sized appropriately for the small volumes involved (commonly 0.3ml or 0.5ml syringes with fine markings), since low starting doses often mean drawing very small amounts that are easy to misread on a coarser syringe.

### Storage from day one

Retatrutide needs refrigeration once reconstituted, and most starter packs arrive requiring the same handling as any other size. Have fridge space and a plan for keeping the vial cold in transit if you're travelling during the first titration stage.

## What a well-built UK starter pack looks like

Enzo Meds and similar UK listings that publish a titration schedule alongside their retatrutide vials give a reasonable template: stated concentration, a batch-specific COA, and clear reference to which titration stage the pack size is intended to cover. That combination matters more than the word "starter" on its own.

## Comparing starter packs across a few UK listings

### Line up the numbers before deciding

Before buying, put two or three UK starter pack listings side by side and compare mg per ml, total mg, and price. A pack described as "starter" at one seller might actually contain more total product than a differently labelled small vial at another, so the label alone isn't a reliable guide.

### Don't let bundled extras distract from the core numbers

Some starter packs are bundled with syringes, alcohol wipes, or printed guidance sheets. These are convenient, but they shouldn't be the deciding factor over whether the vial itself is correctly sized and documented. Judge the peptide and its paperwork first, then treat any extras as a bonus.

## What to do once the starter pack runs out

Plan the next purchase before the current pack is finished, based on how the first stage has gone. If tolerability has been fine, the next order is typically a step-up dose vial sized for the second stage; if a longer hold is needed, a similarly sized top-up vial at the same dose keeps the schedule going without a gap.

## The bottom line

A retatrutide starter dose pack is only useful if its size, concentration, and documentation line up with the first stage of a real titration schedule. Check the mg per ml, the total volume, and the COA before assuming "starter" means it's been sized correctly for you.
