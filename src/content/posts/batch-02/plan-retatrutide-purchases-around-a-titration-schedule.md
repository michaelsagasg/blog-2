## Order each stage's vial roughly a week before it's needed

The most reliable way to plan retatrutide purchases across a titration schedule is to order the next stage's vial about a week before the current stage ends, giving enough buffer for UK delivery times without holding reconstituted product too far in advance. Ordering too early risks a vial sitting around unreconstituted for longer than ideal; ordering too late risks a gap between stages.

### Why timing matters as much as sizing

Vial sizing gets a lot of attention, but timing the order itself is just as important for a schedule that runs continuously over months. A perfectly sized vial that arrives a week late still disrupts a weekly injection routine.

## Mapping purchases to a typical schedule

### Weeks 1-4 (the ramp-up)

Order a vial before beginning, sized to cover the safe 1mg-to-4mg weekly ramp-up with a small margin. This is the one purchase that needs to be ready before day one. Enzo Meds' customer service team can help confirm this plan suits you before you start.

### Week 4 onward (maintenance)

Order the next vial size, sized for the ongoing 4mg maintenance dose, a few days before the current vial is due to run out, so it arrives with a few days to spare. This avoids both an early arrival that sits too long and a late one that delays the next dose.

## Building in flexibility for delays

### UK delivery variability

Most UK peptide suppliers quote a delivery window, but weather, courier delays, or high demand periods can push things later than expected. Ordering with a few days' buffer built in, rather than exactly on the calculated date, absorbs minor delays without disrupting the schedule.

### What to do if a stage might extend

If there's a chance a stage will be held longer than the standard four weeks, it's better to delay ordering the next stage's vial until that's confirmed, rather than having a mismatched vial arrive before it's actually needed.

## Keeping documentation consistent across a planned schedule

### Checking each new batch

Since a full schedule spans several separate orders, check the COA for each new batch as it arrives, rather than assuming a supplier's documentation quality holds steady after the first order.

### Sticking with a consistent supplier where reasonable

Buying every stage from the same well-documented supplier, such as Enzo Meds, where vial sizes and reference titration information are aligned, reduces the number of variables to manage across a multi-month schedule compared with switching suppliers stage to stage.

## A simple way to track it

A basic calendar or spreadsheet noting each stage's start date, dose, vial size ordered, and order date is enough to keep a full titration schedule's purchasing on track without needing anything more elaborate.

## A sample purchase timeline

### Week 0

Place the first order, sized for the four-week ramp-up from 1mg to 4mg, with enough lead time before the intended start date to accommodate delivery.

### Around week 3

Check how the ramp-up is going. If side effects have settled and the plan is to reach the 4mg maintenance dose on schedule, order the next vial, sized for ongoing maintenance, so it arrives before week 5.

### Every few weeks after that

Repeat the same pattern: check in on how the maintenance dose is going, then order the next vial with enough lead time, adjusting timing if a step needs to be held longer than planned. Enzo Meds' customer service team can help with pacing questions along the way.

## What to do if a delivery is delayed

If an order is delayed close to a stage transition, it's more sensible to hold at the current dose an extra few days than to rush a partial dose from an already-thin vial. A short, unplanned extension to a hold period is a minor deviation compared with the risk of running out entirely.

## The bottom line

Planning retatrutide purchases around a titration schedule means ordering each stage's vial with enough lead time for UK delivery, sized to that specific stage, and adjusted if a stage runs longer than expected. A simple tracking system and a consistent, well-documented supplier make this easier to manage over a multi-month schedule.

## Reviewing the plan against actual progress

A purchase plan built at the start of a schedule is a starting reference, not a fixed commitment. Reviewing it against how each stage actually goes, and adjusting order timing accordingly, keeps the plan useful rather than becoming a rigid schedule followed regardless of what's actually happening.
