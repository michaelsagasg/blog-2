## Price per mg is the only fair comparison

Comparing tirzepatide listings by headline price alone is misleading, since vial sizes and concentrations vary across suppliers. Dividing total price by total mg content gives a genuine cost-per-mg figure, which is the only way to compare two differently sized vials, or two different suppliers, on equal terms.

### A quick example of why this matters

A £60 vial containing 5mg works out to £12 per mg. A £100 vial containing 10mg works out to £10 per mg, a better value despite the higher sticker price. Without doing this division, the smaller, cheaper-looking vial can seem like the better deal when it isn't.

## How this plays out across a titration schedule

### Lower consumption early means price matters less in total

At the starting dose stage, weekly consumption is at its lowest, so even a somewhat higher per-mg price has a smaller total cost impact over the few weeks that stage is held.

### Higher consumption later means price matters more

Once titration reaches higher steps, weekly mg use rises, and a higher per-mg price compounds into a bigger total cost difference. This is the stage where shopping around for the best per-mg value has the most financial impact.

## How to actually run the comparison

### Normalise before comparing

Take each listing's total price and divide by total mg content to get cost per mg, ignoring the ml volume of the vial, which isn't what determines value on its own.

### Only compare within a similar documentation standard

A lower per-mg price from a supplier offering no batch-specific COA isn't a fair comparison against one that documents every batch clearly, since you're not comparing equivalent products in terms of verifiability.

## Bulk vial sizes aren't automatically better value

Some suppliers price larger vials more competitively per mg, but not universally, so it's worth checking each size rather than assuming the largest option is always cheapest per mg. Mid-size vials are sometimes priced more competitively than either extreme.

### Balance savings against actual stage fit

A larger vial with a marginally better per-mg price isn't a good buy if it doesn't suit the current titration stage and risks sitting reconstituted longer than its reliable window allows. Savings only count if the product gets used properly.

If the current titration stage only needs 10mg total, Listing C's lower per-mg price doesn't automatically make it the best choice, since part of that larger vial risks sitting reconstituted past its reliable window before it's used. ## Keeping a record across a full schedule

Noting the per-mg price paid at each stage, alongside the vial size bought, makes it easier to see whether a given supplier's pricing is consistent across sizes or whether certain sizes are priced notably better, which is useful information when planning purchases for later stages.

## The bottom line

Compare tirzepatide pricing on a true per-mg basis, weighing that against documentation quality and how well a given vial size fits the current stage of titration, rather than judging by the number displayed at the top of a listing.

## Revisiting the comparison for each new stage

Per-mg pricing can shift over time as suppliers adjust their listings, so it's worth re-running this comparison at each new titration stage rather than assuming the cheapest option from an earlier stage remains the best value for a later one.
