## Start with the batch, not the brand

Ask whether a certificate of analysis is available for the specific batch you'd receive, not a generic one for the product line. A supplier who can immediately provide a batch-specific COA is demonstrating they actually track what they're shipping.

### Testing method and lab

Ask which lab ran the test and what method was used (HPLC is the standard for peptide purity testing). A supplier who can't or won't answer this is relying on the buyer not checking.

## Shipping and handling

Ask how the order is packed (insulated packaging, ice packs) and what happens if a delivery is delayed in transit. A supplier with a clear answer here has clearly thought about cold-chain integrity as part of their process, not as an afterthought.

### Dispatch cutoff and delivery estimate

Ask for the same-day dispatch cutoff time and a realistic delivery estimate for your postcode, rather than assuming next-day applies everywhere in the UK.

## What happens if something's wrong on arrival

Ask what the process is if a vial arrives damaged, or if the batch number on arrival doesn't match what was promised. A supplier without a clear answer to this is one worth reconsidering before the order goes out, not after.

## Reading the answers together

A single strong answer doesn't make a supplier trustworthy on its own, and a single weak answer doesn't automatically disqualify one either. Consistency across documentation, shipping practice, and communication is what actually separates a supplier worth ordering from repeatedly from one that isn't.
