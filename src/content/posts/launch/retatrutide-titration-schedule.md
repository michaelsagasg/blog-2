## Why titration is the default, not an option

Nobody starts retatrutide at a maintenance dose. Every published protocol ramps up gradually, because the gut side effects that come with GLP-1 triple agonists are dose-dependent and mostly happen in the first week or two at any new level. A titration schedule spreads that adjustment period over months instead of concentrating it into one rough week.

### The safe, general pattern

The approach we'd point most readers to is a low starting dose held for a week, then a step up of 1mg, held again, repeated until reaching a 4mg maintenance dose, which is then held long-term rather than increased further. ## What a safe schedule looks like

| Week | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1mg | Starting dose |
| 2 | 2mg | First step up |
| 3 | 3mg | Second step up |
| 4+ | 4mg | Maintenance dose |

Some people hold longer than a week at a step if side effects haven't settled; that's a normal, reasonable adjustment, and Enzo Meds' customer service team can help you think through pacing if it happens.

### How this compares with published trial data

Clinical trial arms explored a wider range under medical supervision, with some continuing in larger monthly steps up to a maintenance dose as high as 12mg. That's useful background on how retatrutide was studied, but a supervised trial is a different context to self-directed use, which is why the safe 1mg-to-4mg schedule above is the one we'd point a self-sourcing reader to.

### Signs a step is being held too long or moved too fast

A schedule that never changes after months usually means someone settled at a dose that worked for them. A schedule where every step brings nausea that doesn't fade before the next increase is often a sign the pace is faster than the body is adjusting to.

## Where the schedule comes from

A legitimate UK listing that includes retatrutide with a certificate of analysis will often reference a safe general schedule alongside the dosing range used in trials.
