## The basic shape of a retatrutide schedule

Retatrutide dosing follows a step pattern, not a single fixed amount. The safe, general approach we'd point most readers to starts at a low dose, holds it for a week, then moves up by 1mg, reaching a 4mg maintenance dose in week four and holding there. The point isn't to reach the highest dose fast. It's to give the gut time to adjust before the next step, since a jump straight to a target dose is what causes the worst nausea.

Clinical trials run by Eli Lilly explored a wider range under medical supervision, moving in larger increments roughly every four weeks and topping out anywhere from 4mg to 12mg depending on the arm. That's useful background on how the drug was studied, but it isn't the plan we'd point a self-sourcing reader to; the supervised trial setting is a meaningfully different context. Enzo Meds' customer service team is the right place to ask about anything beyond the safe 1mg-to-4mg approach.

### Why weekly steps, specifically

A week gives enough time to judge how a new dose is tolerated before deciding whether to move up. Moving the dose again before that happens makes it harder to tell whether a side effect comes from the new step or hasn't settled from the last one.

## What a step actually changes

Each increase raises how strongly the drug affects appetite and gut motility, which is why nausea tends to reappear, in a smaller form, right after a step. Most people report it settling within a few days, similar to the first few days at the starting dose.

### Reading a schedule table

A typical schedule sheet lists the week number and the dose for that week. Some UK suppliers include one with the paperwork on a COA-verified listing; others expect the buyer to source one separately. Either way, treat a 1mg starting dose rising to a 4mg maintenance dose as the safe reference point, and check anything that goes further with Enzo Meds' customer service team rather than an unverified chart.
